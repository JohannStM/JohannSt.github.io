Introduction

One city does not mean one crime pattern

Citywide summaries can make crime look like a single trend, but San Francisco is made up of districts with very different patterns. A useful crime story therefore has to move beyond totals and ask what changes depending on place.

This data story uses data provided by the city of San Francisco from 2003 to 2025 to show how crime patterns differ across police districts, how they are group in one certain areas of the city and how they change ovet the course of the day. The story focuses on 9 crime categories: Arson, Disorderly Conduct, Drug Offenses, Larceny Theft, Missing Person, Prostitution, Robbery, Vandalism and Weapons Laws.

Crime profiles differ sharply across police districts

This first figure introduces the main claim of the story. Instead of just looking at which district has the most crime overall, it compares each district’s profile to the city average for several focus crimes and across two periodsm those two periods are two diffrent data sets from the city of San Francisco. The graphs clearly show that diffrent districts have diffrent crime profiles, for example in the district of Mission Prostitution is much more common than in other districts across the city. The same can be said for Drug Offenses in the district of Tenderloin. The figure also shows that crime profiles have changed over time across the two datasets, for example in the district of Bayview the ratio of Arson has decreased significantly. Now why the changes actually happen between the two is good question. It can be political, social or economic changes in the city. Were new laws made on Drug Offenses? Did the city invest more in certain districts? Did the population change in certain areas? These are all questions that can be explored. Tenderloin has, according to The San Francisco Chronicle, been the center of the city's drug trade, since 2018 having about 60% of all drug offenses. And Mission has a long standing street sex-work corridor.

Figure 2

Mapping crime reveals where patterns concentrate

A district-level map can help show the diffrence in crime profiles across the city and how the location of the district matters when interpreting the data. Figure 2 clearly shows that the district of Tenderloin has by the far the highest ratio of drug offenses relative to the city average, while the district of Bayview has the second highest ratio, but is still far behind. The map also shows that the districts with the highest ratios of drug offenses are all located in the central parts of the city, while the districts in the outskirts have much lower ratios.

Crime also changes over the course of the day

The third and final figure add the time aspect to the story. It shows how crime changes over the course of the day, and how the patterns differ across crime categories. The figure clearly shows that some crimes are more common during the day while others at night. For example drug offenses are most common in the afternoon and early evening, while prostition is most common in the evenins and night. Some crimes are more evenly distributes across the day.
